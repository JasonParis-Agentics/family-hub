Already in the Waveshare box

The 12 V power brick, the two small adapter boards that bridge the monitor to the Pi (one for HDMI, one for Pi power), a short USB-A to USB-C cable for touch, and the screws. You do not need a separate Pi power supply, HDMI cable or case.

Things you probably have

A computer with an SD slot

Or any USB microSD reader (~$10). Used once, to flash the card with Raspberry Pi Imager.

An outlet near the spot

One plug for the whole thing. Plan a cord path; the brick is small enough to hide behind the screen or arm.

Home WiFi

The Pi joins on first boot. The hub keeps working offline from its last saved copy if the WiFi drops.

A small Phillips screwdriver

For the cooler, the Pi standoffs and the back cover.

How it connects

Build order

  1. Flash the card. Raspberry Pi Imager → Raspberry Pi OS (64-bit, desktop). In its settings set hostname familyhub, a user, your WiFi, and paste your SSH key.
  2. Prep the Pi. Card in, cooler on (the two push-pins sit diagonally), fan cable into the FAN header.
  3. Mount it in the monitor. Pi onto the compartment standoffs, both adapter boards plugged in, short USB cable to the port marked TOUCH, 12 V brick into the monitor.
  4. Deploy the hub. Edit app/data.js with your kids, chores and rewards, then npx wrangler deploy. The README walks through it.
  5. Turn it into a kiosk. One command over SSH runs pi/setup_kiosk.sh: auto-login, full-screen browser, no screen blanking, nightly 4 a.m. refresh.
  6. Hang it. Landscape. Put the screen's center around 47–50" off the floor so the youngest kid can reach the bottom row.
